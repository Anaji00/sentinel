# Frontend research: what to improve next (12 Nov 2026)

Scope: read-only audit of `frontend/src` against web research. Nothing was changed. I read `CandleChart.tsx`, `TickerHero.tsx`, `Desk.tsx`, `ComparePanel.tsx`, `OrderTicket.tsx`, `ui/Sidebar.tsx`, `app/(dashboard)/page.tsx` and the top of `IdeaCard.tsx`, and grepped the others for jargon. I did **not** view the live pages, so wording findings come from source strings.

## How strong is the evidence? Read this first

The search tool returned mostly blogs and vendor pages. Published usability research on *stock pages* barely exists in what I could reach. So:

- **Strong (peer-reviewed or regulator):** Barber/Huang/Odean/Schwarz on Robinhood-style simple displays and attention trading; forecast-communication research (Raftery review; Northwestern election-forecast study); SEC Marketing Rule commentary on hypothetical performance.
- **Medium:** NN/g on progressive disclosure and confirmation dialogs (I saw only fragments, not full text); WCAG success criteria 1.4.1 and 1.1.1 (reached through university and vendor guides, not the W3C text).
- **Weak (opinion, often vendor):** candle/volume/average conventions, dashboard hierarchy, "AI slop" tells, product comparisons. I flag these in the table. Where a finding rests only on weak sources, it is also supported by something visible in our own code, and the table says so.
- **Not found:** any study on order-confirmation screens as a cause of retail errors, any study on mobile trading apps, any authoritative crosshair spec. Those points are reasoned from general UX practice, not cited.

## What the research says (short)

1. **Charts.** Candles for daily data, volume as bars under the price on the same time axis, 20/50/200-bar averages, few overlays, timeframe switcher; too many indicators slow decisions. Sources (weak, all secondary): [Nevron](https://www.nevron.com/dotnet-stock-chart), [CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/knowledge/trading-investing/how-to-read-stock-charts/), [Pepperstone](https://pepperstone.com/en/learn-to-trade/trading-guides/how-to-read-financial-market-charts/). TradingView's own docs list the chart types and timeframes ([charting library docs](https://tradingview.com/charting-library-docs/v28/getting_started/Key-Features), [Supercharts](https://tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000746464)).
2. **Hierarchy.** What shows first signals importance; defer the rest ([NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/alertbox/progressive-disclosure.html)). Practitioner guides say put the answer to the main question first and drill-downs lower ([Lollypop](https://lollypop.design/blog/2026/may/investment-dashboard-ux-design-guide/), opinion).
3. **Simple lists can mislead.** Robinhood users showed more attention-driven buying, and the top daily-bought stocks averaged -4.7% abnormal return over 20 days; the authors tie it partly to the app's simplified display of stock lists ([Barber, Huang, Odean, Schwarz, J. Finance 2022](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:77:y:2022:i:6:p:3141-3190); [SSRN copy](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/SSRN-id3715077.pdf)). SEC and FINRA have pursued "digital engagement" nudges ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/08/27/sec-steps-up-research-into-gamification-of-trading-with-online-brokers-gary-gensler-says.html)). Lesson for us: a ranked list of "top ideas" is attention bait unless it carries the evidence beside it.
4. **Probabilities and track records.** Calibration drives trust; adding uncertainty did not confuse people; simple formats beat ranges for cognitive load ([Raftery, arXiv 1408.4812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.4812)); in election-forecast sites, trust grew over repeated cycles of honest, calibrated forecasts, and text summaries plus quantile dotplots earned the most trust, while calibration graphics had small effects ([Northwestern, 2024](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/files/2024-dice.pdf), simulated forecasts, so read with care). For back-tested or model-portfolio results, regulators want net-of-cost figures, out-of-sample tests, and no cherry-picking ([GIPS slides on the SEC Marketing Rule](https://www.gipsstandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/sec-marketing-rule-latest-dev-and-practical-solutions-slides-02-24-2026.pdf); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/-/media/files/publication/morgan-lewis-title/white-paper/2021/the-secs-new-marketing-rule-key-takeaways-for-advisers.pdf); law-firm commentary, not rule text). Sentinel is a personal paper-trading tool, so the rule does not bind it, but its standards are a good honesty bar.
5. **Order entry.** Confirmation dialogs lose value through overuse ("dialog fatigue") ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/), fragments only; [Primer](https://primer.style/product/components/confirmation-dialog/guidelines)). A hold-to-confirm or armed step beats "Are you sure?" ([DEV](https://dev.to/tomj/why-holding-buttons-is-superior-to-confirmation-dialogs-in-ux-design-1fic), opinion). No study on trading tickets found.
6. **Accessibility.** Colour must not be the only carrier of meaning (WCAG 1.4.1); charts need a text alternative or data table (1.1.1) ([Penn State](https://accessibility.psu.edu/guidelines/charts), [UW-Madison](https://it.wisc.edu/learn/accessible-content-tech/accessible-data-visualizations), [Smashing](https://shop.smashingmagazine.com/2024/02/accessibility-standards-empower-better-chart-visual-design/)). Check against the [W3C text](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) before claiming compliance.
7. **Tools compared (all review blogs, several with vendor ties; weak).** TradingView: best charting and alerts, watchlist-wide alerts. Finviz: screener and heat map. Yahoo Finance: simplest free start. Koyfin: fundamentals and macro dashboards. StockAnalysis.com: fast, ad-light statements and tables. Sources: [Stock Rover](https://www.stockrover.com/?p=46612), [ChartingLens](https://chartinglens.com/blog/best-koyfin-alternatives), [Bullish Bears](https://bullishbears.com/koyfin-review/), [TradingView features](https://www.tradingview.com/features). Takeaway: none of them wins on "plain words"; that is our opening. Each wins by doing one job fast (chart, scan, statements). We should copy the *job-first* layout, not the features.
8. **Signs of AI-generated UI and copy (opinion from design blogs, consistent across them):** emoji as icons, purple/indigo gradients and glows, three-card feature rows, generic buttons ("Get Started", "Learn More"), slogan headlines, vague lists that cover every benefit and take no stance, invented statistics, placeholder text, more effects than needed. Sources: [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/built-with-ai-trust-signals-2026), [Fountain Institute](https://pages.thefountaininstitute.com/posts/7-tells-that-a-ui-is-ai-generated), [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [GIGAZINE](https://www.gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260423-design-slop). No measured data except one unverified 28% gradient figure.

## Backend data the frontend needs that does not exist yet

I did not read the backend; this list is what the findings above call for. Check each against `/radar`, `/predictions` and `/portfolio` before building.

1. **Per-idea track record:** count of past calls, share right, average result after costs, split live vs back-test, for the same horizon and tier as the idea. (Findings 1, 2.)
2. **Return spread for each call:** a typical range (say 20th to 80th percentile of past outcomes for calls like this), not just a point estimate. (Finding 5.)
3. **Bar-count by date range:** an endpoint that takes a range (1M, 1Y, 5Y) and returns the right bar size, plus 200-bar average warm-up bars before the window. (Findings 3, 4.)
4. **Quote with age:** last price, bid, ask, timestamp and a "delayed by N minutes" flag per symbol. (Findings 7, 15.)
5. **Pre-trade account facts:** buying power, cash after the order, current position in the symbol, estimated slippage for a market order. (Finding 7.)
6. **Alerts store:** per-user alert rules (price cross, new filing, new call, model change) and a notification feed. (Finding 8.)
7. **Fundamentals snapshot:** market cap, P/E, dividend yield, 52-week range, next earnings date, from free sources. (Finding 21.)
8. **Screener endpoint:** one list of stocks with day change, volume vs usual, model view and next event, sortable and filterable server-side. (Finding 20.)
9. **Paper account summary for the header:** equity and day change, cheap to poll. (Finding 22.)
10. **One price source of truth** shared by the hero, chart and compare panel. (Finding 13.)
