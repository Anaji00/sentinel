# Keep the Boring Book, Test Its Shorts

The evidence favours what Sentinel already runs: a slow, diversified, cross-sectional stock book, with most remaining value in measuring costs, testing the short leg and hardening validation, not in new algorithms. Combined equity anomalies net somewhere between about 20 bps and 1.42% a month depending on assumptions, and the low end is the better prior, because the higher figure comes from an unreplicated working paper. Autonomous LLM traders fail their strongest independent re-test, RL has no independent net-of-cost replication, and scheduling algorithms solve an impact problem that orders of about $550 do not have. The largest open risk is the short leg: published work finds anomaly returns concentrated in costly shorts, and Sentinel's own trial record shows its buys lagging the market after beta, while its backtest screens borrow with today's list rather than a point-in-time one. The first measured rebuild traded about $171k, so each 10 bps of cost is worth about 2 points of equity a year if that pace holds, against only 0.2-0.3 points recovered by a 25% drift band. Top priorities are a shadow-cost ledger on the fills recorded from today, a short-leg attribution with a point-in-time borrow proxy, B1 across calendar offsets, and written halt and retirement rules. No forward record can confirm this model soon: at a true Sharpe of 0.75, 95% confidence takes about six years. Nothing below changes sizing or execution without your approval, and most source figures were read at abstract or summary level.

**Evidence labels.** [Primary] means a regulator, official documentation or a fund's own page. [Paper] means a published paper read at abstract or extract level. [Preprint] means unreviewed. [Secondary] means a digest or press report. [Vendor] means a seller's or interested party's claim. [Sentinel measured] means your supplied figures or the repo's trial record. [My calculation] and [Inference] are mine and carry no outside evidence.

## Only low-turnover stock selection survives at this size

Every family's net edge is far below its headline, and replication does not mean tradability. McLean and Pontiff find returns 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication across 97 predictors [Secondary] ([Boston College](https://www.bc.edu/content/bc-web/schools/carroll-school/news/2017/pontiff-paper.html)). Chen and Velikov put the average anomaly at **4 bps a month** after spreads, decay and the modern era, with combinations near 20 bps [Paper] ([JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/zeroing-in-on-the-expected-returns-of-anomalies/945133D5A3ECEEAF466AEE91551FD225)). Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen's roughly 85% replication rate is not a contradiction, since it asks whether factors exist gross, not whether they can be harvested today [Secondary] ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)). Chen and Zimmermann add that publication bias explains only 10-15% of in-sample returns, which points to trading and costs as the main source of decay rather than false discovery [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13623)).

Cross-sectional ML remains the best fit. Gu, Kelly and Xiu report trees and neural networks leading, driven by momentum, liquidity and volatility signals [Paper] ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398)). Avramov and coauthors find profits attenuate considerably without microcaps, distressed names or volatile episodes [Secondary] ([Hebrew University](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/machine-learning-vs-economic-restrictions-evidence-from-stock-ret/)). The 1.42% a month net figure comes through a vendor digest of a working paper, so treat it as unreplicated [Secondary] ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-expected-returns-of-machine-learning-strategies/)). Sentinel's restriction to the 1,000 most liquid common stocks already addresses the microcap problem, though not necessarily the distress one ([Sentinel measured: `MODEL_TRIALS.md`]).

Firm lore adds less than it appears to. Medallion's roughly 66% gross average for 1988-2018 traces to a book and has no public audit, and Renaissance itself attributes the gap to its outside funds to holding period and other differences [Secondary] ([Institutional Investor](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b1k2fymby99nj0/Famed-Medallion-Fund-Stretches-Explanation-to-the-Limit-Professor-Claims)). The closer comparison is AQR's public market-neutral fund: **26.53% in 2025 but 6.97% annualised over ten years** [Primary] ([AQR](https://funds.aqr.com/funds/aqr-equity-market-neutral-fund)). That spread warns against judging a factor book on any recent window. The firm lessons with hard documentation are about process. The SEC found Two Sigma left known model vulnerabilities unaddressed from March 2019 to August 2023, and one employee changed more than a dozen models without supervision [Primary] ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-15)).

Open-source projects offer ideas, not a platform. All seven reviewed repositories were active on 5 November 2026, but none was found publishing audited live results, and that search was not targeted [Primary for activity] ([Freqtrade](https://github.com/freqtrade/freqtrade); [Qlib](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib); [NautilusTrader](https://github.com/nautechsystems/nautilus_trader)). Three ideas transfer cheaply using existing dependencies: Freqtrade's truncated-history look-ahead test ([docs](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/lookahead-analysis/)), Qlib's record of which model was online when ([docs](https://qlib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/component/online.html)), and Nautilus's broker reconciliation on restart ([docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/latest/concepts/overview/)). Nautilus's "production-grade" label is the project's own, and US equity support in Lean and Nautilus was not confirmed.

## LLMs earn a place as feature extractors, not as traders

The headline agent results rest on very small tests. TradingAgents reports Sharpes of 5.6-8.2 over about three months on three large stocks, with no costs stated, and I found no independent re-test [Preprint, unreplicated] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.20138v7)). FinMem reported +61.78% on TSLA at Sharpe 2.68 [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.13743)). FINSABER then retested over 2004-2024 with a survivorship-aware universe. It got **Sharpe 0.4-0.9 on the same TSLA window, no significant alpha (all p above 0.34), and significant underperformance against buy-and-hold** [Paper, KDD '26] ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078)). The authors admit look-ahead is not fully eliminated but argue leakage would favour the LLMs.

Live contests measure entertainment more than skill. Alpha Arena is about two short seasons with few models and reportedly heavy leverage, and its details reach me only through commercial blogs that conflict on Season 1.5's end date and return, so treat all of it as [Vendor] ([TradeRank](https://www.traderank.ai/blog/alpha-arena-alternatives-2026); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/ai-model-grok-4-2-triumphs-in-trading-tournament/)). StockBench's four-month range of -2.8% to +2.5% has no costs modelled, and its abstract and table disagree on how many models beat buy-and-hold [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209)). LiveTradeBench's useful finding is that general model rankings do not predict trading results, so a bigger model is no reason to expect better P&L [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.03628)).

The best positive result is narrower and decays. Lopez-Lira and Tang test headline scoring after GPT-4's cutoff across 159,137 firm-headline-dates. Tradable next-day drift has a pre-cost Sharpe of 2.97, but **the Sharpe falls from 6.54 in late 2021 to 1.22 in early 2024, and the strategy is unprofitable at 20 bps round-trip** [Preprint; no journal listed] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). It is also stronger in small stocks and reliant on low-cost shorting, the two things this book cannot count on. Memorised outcomes contaminate in-sample tests, so only post-cutoff results count [Preprint] ([LAP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23847)). Glasserman and Lin found distraction by company identity outweighing look-ahead in their setting, which supports testing an anonymised variant [Paper] ([JFDS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17322)). Local fit is modest: Llama2's drift Sharpe was 0.97 and possibly overstated by its later cutoff, and FinBERT's was -0.33 [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)). Llama-3.1 extraction lifted F1 only from 0.576 to 0.600 on top of FinBERT, with no return test on the abstract page [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28496)). TradingAgents' 11 LLM calls per prediction also conflict with Sentinel's rule that inference is the scarcest resource.

RL ranks lowest for this system. A review of 167 articles says implementation quality and domain knowledge often outweigh algorithmic complexity [Preprint] ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.10913)), and a 2026 intraday review finds costs handled inconsistently [Paper] ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/20/10060)). Positive single studies exist ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.04755)), but no independent FinRL-style replication with realistic costs was found. That absence comes from a bounded search, and FINSABER's RL rows were not extracted.

## Costs and shorts matter more than execution cleverness

**The measured stakes.** The 29 October rebuild traded about $171k [Sentinel measured]. Twelve rebuilds a year at that pace is about $2.05M, so **each 10 bps of all-in cost is about 2 points of equity a year**, or about 0.17% a month [My calculation]. With legs of about $50k, full replacement of both legs would trade $200k, so $171k implies about 86% replacement [My calculation]. That is well above the 50% one-sided monthly line under which Novy-Marx and Velikov find most anomalies keep net spreads [Paper] ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)). This is one observation, soon after the 24 October switch to B1, and it may include transition trading, so it needs later rebuilds before it means anything. B1's backtest shows +0.64% a month net at 10 bps a side [Sentinel measured: `MODEL_TRIALS.md`]; I did not trace whether that return shares the live account's equity base, so the two numbers are not yet directly comparable.

**Bands are weaker here than the literature suggests, but one version is untested.** Novy-Marx and Velikov call the buy/hold spread the most effective simple mitigation [Paper] ([NBER](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)). Sentinel's measured result for a 25% drift band is only about 0.2-0.3 points a year at 10 bps, on simulated drift of 2.4% of the book a session from proxy signals [Sentinel measured, simulated]. That is roughly a tenth of the cost at stake, and it overrides the research notes' enthusiasm for weight-drift bands. A drift band and a rank buffer are different tools, though. The trial engine's quintile book uses a `HOLD_BUFFER` of 0.40 (`shared/utils/factor_model.py`), while live `b1_book` takes only percentiles and a borrowable set, with no holdings input (`shared/utils/auto_trade.py`). If the 86% figure persists, a rank buffer on B1 attacks the rebuild itself and is the better test. Qlib's TopK-dropout is a related cap on replacements ([docs](https://qlib.readthedocs.io/en/latest/component/workflow.html)). Azevedo and coauthors caution that most mitigation techniques cut turnover and net return together [Secondary, unreplicated] ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-expected-returns-of-machine-learning-strategies/)), so judge on net Sharpe across offsets, not on turnover.

**Scheduling algorithms have no job to do.** TWAP, VWAP, POV and Almgren-Chriss trade impact against timing risk ([Almgren-Chriss](https://quantitativebrokers.com/s/Optimal-Execution-of-Portfolio-Transaction-_-AlmgrenChriss-1999.pdf)). AQR's $1.7 trillion of live executions show cost driven by size relative to volume [Paper via secondary] ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Working-Paper/Trading-Costs); [ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-trading-cost-truths)). AQR's bps do not transfer, because its algorithm supplies liquidity and rarely pays the spread [Secondary] ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/the-best-research-paper-ever-written-on-trading-costs/)). Per-name orders near $550 are close to no participation at all, so spread and time of day are the live levers [Inference]. Closing auctions are not free: prices can deviate and reverse overnight, per working-paper and conference material [Paper, unpublished] ([AEA](https://aeaweb.org/conference/2021/preliminary/paper/H9T4hef7)). Alpaca's `cls` order has a 15:50 ET cutoff and auction orders must be whole-share [Primary] ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/orders-at-alpaca)), which matters at these sizes. No retrieved study compares open, midday and close for small US orders, and the commonly quoted open-versus-midday figures could not be attributed, so I omit them.

**The fill record bounds some costs, not all.** Fill recording began 5 November, so it does not cover the 29 October rebuild, and by my calendar count the first recorded rebuild falls around 30 November. Paper fills come from live quotes without impact, queue or latency, and omit regulatory fees, dividends and borrow fees [Primary] ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)). So the record is a floor on impact and those omitted charges. It is not a strict floor on spread, because paper also omits price improvement, which could make it pessimistic for small marketable orders [Inference]. Passive-first savings cannot be evaluated on paper, since limits fill only once marketable. Any such "saving" measured here should be discarded.

**The short leg is the largest threat.** Across 162 anomalies, Muravyev, Pearson and Pollet report **0.14% a month before short-sale costs and -0.01% after borrow fees**, with high-fee stocks supplying the bulk of momentum and profitability returns [Paper, abstract and summaries] ([NBER draft](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f184100/f184100.pdf); [CXO](https://cxoadvisory.com/short-selling/shorting-costs-kill-stock-return-anomalies)). Sentinel has three relevant facts. Live B1 already excludes names not easy to borrow and those above the fee limit (`services/agents/auto_trader.py`), and Alpaca charges no borrow fee on its easy-to-borrow list [Primary] ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/margin-and-short-selling)). Yet the backtest's filter is marked "shortable and easy to borrow today" (`services/agents/factor_engine.py`), so an 11-year test uses a current snapshot, and the direction of that bias is not established [Inference]. And the adopted P1+P2 quintile configuration shows **buys at -0.57% a month against the market after beta** while earning +1.03% net [Sentinel measured: `MODEL_TRIALS.md`]. That implies a return reliant on shorts, though that is the quintile book and B1's split was not found in the record. Alpaca's 100-share locate lots also make hard-to-borrow names uneconomic at about $525 per short [Primary; Inference on economics].

**Fails-to-deliver fit here, as a risk input only.** The 21-session IC of -0.017 (t -4.8) comes with an insignificant top-minus-bottom spread and survivorship bias [Sentinel measured]. The IC is under half the model's roughly 0.037, and a strong t with no spread means the information does not reach the tails where a book trades. If the IC windows overlap, the t is also overstated; I did not check how it was computed. Heavy fails plausibly mark names that are difficult to borrow [Inference, untested]. Their negative IC could then be the same trap Muravyev describes: return available on paper from shorts you cannot hold cheaply. So test it first as a free borrow-stress proxy and short-side exclusion, measure B1's overlap, and do not add it to the ranker on this evidence. Any test counts as another logged trial.

**Equal weight stays, and Sentinel's own trials agree with the literature.** DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal found no optimiser consistently beating 1/N, although in asset allocation rather than alpha-sorted books [Paper] ([RFS](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:22:y:2009:i:5:p:1915-1953)). In Sentinel's B-series, B1 reached net Sharpe 0.82, against 0.31 for calibrated return over variance, -0.01 with beta-neutral legs, 0.14 with stress scaling and -0.09 with 15% short stops [Sentinel measured, one calendar]. Noise of 0.4 from timing cannot explain the worst of those gaps, although it can explain smaller ones. The stop result fits Kaminski and Lo, who show stop-losses cost expected return under a random walk [Paper] ([MIT](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/bb69ca4b-0cdc-487f-831d-63b2e84fafee)). Vol-managed results are disputed: real-time versions lost to unmanaged portfolios across 103 cases [Secondary] ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/)), while a conditional multifactor version survived costs [Paper] ([JF](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/3716/1/The%20Journal%20of%20Finance%20-%202024%20-%20DeMIGUEL%20-%20A%20Multifactor%20Perspective%20on%20Volatility%E2%80%90Managed%20Portfolios.pdf)). HRP's support is low quality ([FGV](https://periodicos.fgv.br/rbfin/article/view/89848)). One correction to the research notes: at legs of 50% each, gross is about 100% of equity, so their warning about sitting at Alpaca's 2x overnight limit does not apply.

## Backtests predict little, and live monitoring cannot rescue them quickly

Quantopian's 888 algorithms remain the closest evidence for independent builders: backtest Sharpe explained under 2.5% of out-of-sample Sharpe, more backtesting went with a larger shortfall, and volatility and drawdown persisted far better than return [Paper; platform authors, short windows] ([Wiecki et al.](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/208/All%20that%20Glitters%20Is%20Not%20Gold%20-%20Comparing%20Backtest%20and%20Out-of-Sample%20Performance%20on%20a%20Large%20Cohort%20o.pdf)). Its short windows inflate the bleakness, and it does not show that a disciplined single-model process has no predictive value.

Sentinel's record shows a selection and timing problem. A one-day calendar change moved net Sharpe from 1.01 to 0.61, though that run also had one more day of data [Sentinel measured: `MODEL_TRIALS.md`]. The live inputs' 0.97 mean across four offsets (1.24, 0.86, 1.08, 0.70) was selected on the same offsets it is reported on. It also describes the buffered quintile book, not B1. The 0.82 for B1 comes from a single calendar on the earlier forecasts, and I did not find a B1 offset table in the record, which makes that the first gap to close. Timing-luck research reports up to about 350 bp of CAGR dispersion by rebalance date and a roughly 1/N reduction from tranching, but costs can outweigh it for small accounts [Secondary] ([Newfound](https://www.thinknewfound.com/rebalance-timing-luck); [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/the-tranching-dilemma/)). Fixing the model and shifting only the trade date, then the reverse, separates execution luck from model instability, and it is the cheapest valuable experiment available [Inference].

The deflated-Sharpe gate deserves to stay exactly as recorded, because it is the protection that fails most easily. It reads 0.96, 0.89 or 0.35 depending on how trials and variance are counted [Sentinel measured: `MODEL_TRIALS.md`], and Bailey and Lopez de Prado require independent trials, with an implied count derivable from the correlation between trials [Paper] ([DSR](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). Friendly replication evidence for published factors does not excuse a private search over features and books. Report 12 and 41 trials side by side, score the gate per offset, and add PBO over logged trial returns ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Published PBO cut-offs conflict, so treat any threshold as a convention. CPCV's claimed superiority rests mainly on one synthetic-data study [Paper, unreplicated] ([preprint copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/725401650/SSRN-id4778909)). Keep walk-forward as the performance record and use CPCV only on finalists; 45 fits per configuration at C(10,2) is expensive [My calculation].

Survivorship remains partly open. Free data covers about four in ten delisted exchange-listed stocks (`services/collector-tradfi/main.py`) [Sentinel measured, via notes]. Shumway and Warther's -55% for missing Nasdaq performance delistings is old and Nasdaq-specific, so use it as a stress value rather than an estimate [Paper] ([JF](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:54:y:1999:i:6:p:2361-2379)). Whether terminal delisting losses reach the backtest was not traced.

**Monitoring power is the hard limit** [My calculation from the notes' normal approximation; autocorrelation lengthens these].

| True annual Sharpe | 80% confidence | 95% confidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | about 13 months | about 4.1 years |
| 0.75 | about 19 months | about 6.2 years |
| 0.50 | about 3.2 years | about 12.2 years |

At an IC of 0.037 with monthly SD near 0.119, a healthy model shows a negative 12-month mean IC about 14% of the time, so rolling IC is a dashboard item and not a stop rule. A CUSUM-style sequential test is the established form for retirement decisions ([factorAnalytics](https://rdrr.io/github/sangeeuw/factorAnalytics/man/cusumActMgr.html)), but no evidence was found for specific thresholds. For planning, a 25-50% haircut ([Falck et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.01380)) takes B1's 0.82 to about 0.4-0.6 [My calculation; published-factor analogy only]. Fast halts should therefore rest on data and operations, retirement on years of pre-registered evidence. Those rules are best written now, since the B1 forward test is already running and later rules invite tuning.

## Retail base rates and failures point to controls, not more signals

Measured base rates are poor but describe a different activity. In Brazil, 97% of day traders who persisted over 300 days lost money [Paper] ([Chague et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:spa:wpaper:2019wpecon47)). In Taiwan, under 1% reliably earned net abnormal returns [Paper] ([Barber et al.](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:finmar:v:18:y:2014:i:c:p:1-24)). In India, 93% of about 11.3 million individual derivatives traders lost money over FY22-FY24 [Regulator study via secondary] ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-study-shows-93-percent-individual-fo-traders-incur-losses.html)). **I found no audited dataset of retail bot users, so any claimed bot success rate is unmeasured**, and marketplace pages are [Vendor] marketing ([MQL5 example](https://www.mql5.com/en/market/product/85076)). The CFTC warns that AI does not make bots money machines and treats guaranteed returns as a fraud signal [Primary] ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html)).

Failures fall into two families with different remedies. Knight lost over $460M in about 45 minutes after one of eight servers missed a deploy, dead code reactivated through a repurposed flag, 97 warning emails went unread, and limits were not linked to order entry [Primary] ([SEC order](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/admin/2013/34-70694.pdf)). Crowded unwinds have no code fix: August 2007 hit quant long-short funds through deleveraging, though its authors later called parts of that explanation speculative [Paper] ([Khandani and Lo](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14465)). In 2020, AQR's market-neutral fund was down 19% by mid-November and Renaissance's public funds lost 19-31% [Secondary] ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-11-17/renaissance-two-sigma-see-losses-as-quant-giants-navigate-chaos)). Dollar neutral is not risk neutral [Preprint] ([Kakushadze](https://arxivlens.com/PaperView/ViewPdf/quant-bust-2020-8042-2057682)). FINRA's Notice 15-09 gives a usable checklist: change management, kill switch, phased pilots, reconciliation [Primary, written for firms] ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/15-09)).

Much of this already exists. `auto_trader.py` has a kill switch checked before orders, a drift benchmark, a per-name weight cap, a 1% ADV liquidity cap and a rule keeping the old book under 800 scored names, so the memory note saying these remain to build looks stale; I read only that part of the code and did not audit it. For a monthly book the characteristic failure is one wrong book executed in full, so the remaining value lies in plausibility checks on the target book and in reconciling intended, submitted and filled quantities [Inference].

Squeeze risk is bounded per name and real in aggregate. Each short is about 1.05% of the short leg, or about 0.53% of equity at 50% legs. A GME-size 1,600% close-to-close move untrimmed costs **about 8.4% of equity**, 2,700% about 14.2%, and a 20% rally in the whole short leg about 10% before any long offset [My calculation; [SEC staff report](https://www.sec.gov/files/staff-report-equity-options-market-struction-conditions-early-2021.pdf)]. The correlated case is the worse one: Goldman's most-shorted basket reportedly rose 98% in three months, though that basket was selected for high short interest [Secondary, sell-side] ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/markets/reddit-traders-could-keep-terrorizing-wall-street-goldman-sachs)). Paper hides recalls and buy-ins, and D'Avolio's older data show a mean 23 trading days to re-establish after a recall [Paper] ([D'Avolio](https://www.johnhcochrane.com/s/davolio_borrowing_stock_JF.pdf)). B1's overlap with heavily shorted names is unmeasured.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the question from "which algorithm should Sentinel add?" to "does B1's edge survive its own shorts and its own turnover?" The notable finding is how far the repo's evidence moves the outside advice: the measured drift band undercuts the most-recommended fix in the form tried, today's-list borrow screening and weak buys sharpen the most dangerous literature finding, and the B-series supplies direct evidence against optimisers and stops that the literature only offers by analogy. What stays unknown is B1's offset distribution, its long and short split, typical rebuild turnover, and real spread paid.

Paper can answer the first three, and only partly the fourth. Paper trading here is therefore best viewed as a test of process and plumbing, plus a floor on some costs, not as evidence of profit. At 0.4-0.6 planning Sharpe, even a clean multi-year record will stay statistically ambiguous, so the gate before any real capital should combine the frozen DSR, the short-leg result and a measured cost gap, and that decision remains yours.
