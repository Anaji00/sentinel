# Manuals Add Little; Borrow Decides B1

Manuals, named algorithms and competitions add less edge than process: they supply one verified order-handling fix, a cost budget in Sharpe units, factor attribution and a labelling rule for the record, but no method with net-of-cost evidence on a monthly gradient-boosted long-short US book. The largest change since the earlier report comes from Sentinel's own 5 November entries, not from any book. B1's net Sharpe is 0.49 on simple returns rather than 1.23 on log, it narrowly fails the registered 0.5 test, and that figure uses no borrow filter; restricting shorts to names at or under 5% fee cuts it to 0.19 with fees charged. Rank buffers, top 1% inclusion, crowded-short and volatile-short drops, and vol-sized legs have all failed registered tests, so the most-recommended book tricks are now spent. The best remaining work is to fix order-status recording before the forward record starts on 30 November, attribute the short leg's return to factors, rescore the choices made on log returns, and build a dated borrow-stress proxy. Avoid meta-labeling, triple barrier, Kelly sizing and contest methods as model changes. Reading depth is thin: **no book was read in full, several were seen only as a table of contents**, and most papers were read at abstract or summary level. Nothing here changes sizing or execution without your approval.

**Evidence labels.** [Primary] is a regulator, official documentation or organiser page, which does not make a self-description independently verified. [Paper] and [Preprint] are read at abstract or extract level unless stated. [Author] is an author's own paper, blog or repo. [TOC], [Blurb] and [Review] describe indirect book access. [Secondary] is a digest or press report. [Vendor] and [Self-reported] are interested-party claims. [Anecdote] is a forum or blog post. [Sentinel measured] is `MODEL_TRIALS.md` or code read today. [My calculation] and [Inference] are mine and carry no outside evidence.

## The manuals agree with Sentinel and were barely read

The honest limit on this section is depth. What follows is mostly tables of contents, reviews and authors' own papers, so it cannot support claims about what these books argue in detail.

|---|---|---|

| Lopez de Prado, *Advances in Financial ML* | TOC; his "10 Reasons" paper headings and opening pages | Moderate on the paper, none on the book |

Within those limits, three additions look useful. Carver, via notes, measures cost in Sharpe units and caps it near one third of expected gross Sharpe ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24243)) [Secondary], and his own post nets holding costs into that limit ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2020/04/how-fast-should-we-trade.html)) [Author]. Applied to B1, trading at the observed $171k a rebuild or the backtest's 61% turnover costs about 0.13-0.18 Sharpe, and the flat borrow estimate about 0.23. Against gross of about 0.83-0.88, **total cost is roughly 43-47% of gross, with trading alone at only 15-21%** [My calculation; rough, and it mixes runs]. Borrow, which paper cannot observe, is what breaches the heuristic, and it comes from futures work, not a proof.

Second, Paleologo's reviewer separates factor from idiosyncratic P&L, then idiosyncratic P&L into selection, sizing and timing, relaying anecdotal evidence of mostly selection skill ([review](https://chasingthevig.substack.com/p/advanced-portfolio-management-book)) [Review]. That matters because removing the most volatile fifth of shorts takes Sharpe from 0.47 to -0.01. Stambaugh, Yu and Yuan find the idiosyncratic-volatility effect negative among overpriced stocks and stronger where shorting is harder ([JF abstract](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v70y2015i5p1903-1948.html)) [Paper]. So Sentinel's pattern looks like documented mispricing, located where borrow is worst [Inference]. `exposure_lens_job.py` reports the book's net beta and macro exposures, which is not a return attribution, so this gap stands [Sentinel measured].

Third, the generalised fundamental law, IR = TC x IC x root breadth ([Clarke et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v58y2002i5p48-66.html)) [Paper], gives a useful accounting check and no forecast. B1's rank-to-weight correlation is about 0.68, against 0.70 with the top 1% included, which matches that carve-out's trivial +0.02 [My calculation]. An IC of 0.037 and gross Sharpe near 0.85 imply about 44-96 independent bets a month against 168-190 names held, an order-of-magnitude reading that uses a log-era IC [My calculation]. Positions probably share common bets, which again favours attribution.

The manuals' sizing advice argues against more risk. Carver and Chan both treat full Kelly as too aggressive ([the7circles](https://the7circles.uk/?p=24214)) [Secondary]; ([Chan](https://epchan.blogspot.com/2010/04/how-do-you-limit-drawdown-using-kelly.html?m=1)) [Author]. The account runs near 11% volatility, inside the 8-18% that a marked-down half-Kelly suggests, and its worst month of about -13% is roughly four monthly standard deviations [My calculation]. Where the books disagree, Sentinel's trials are better evidence: walk-forward (criticised by [Lopez de Prado](https://www.garp.org/hubfs/Whitepapers/a1Z1W0000054x6lUAA.pdf) [Author], core for [Jansen](https://github.com/stefan-jansen/machine-learning-for-trading) [Author]) is resolved by keeping it as the record, and stops by the -0.09 on 15% short stops, a log-era figure on the B3 base.

The failed buffer is conditional, not absolute. Isichenko's preprint finds no-trade bands widening with cost and argues modest execution gains matter when alpha and costs nearly balance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15239v1)) [Author], which fits +0.01 at 10 bps and +0.08 at 20. Novy-Marx and Velikov's preference for buy/hold spreads ([RFS](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:oup:rfinst:v:29:y:2016:i:1:p:104-147.)) [Paper] is therefore not refuted. Halving turnover from 61% to 32% would save about 0.06 Sharpe at 10 bps, so the buffer appears to give back about 0.05 of signal, and clearing the +0.10 rule would need costs near 25 bps a side. This assumes the best result came at the widest buffer and that 61% is a share of $200k, neither confirmed [My calculation]. One re-test is justified only if measured costs reach that area.

## Regulatory detail mostly does not bind, with a few exceptions

FINRA Notice 26-10 replaces the pattern-day-trader designation and $25,000 minimum with an intraday margin standard, effective 4 June 2026 with phase-in to 20 October 2027 ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/26-10)) [Primary]; a monthly book at about 100% gross barely touches it. The exceptions are concrete. Shorts at $5 or more carry the greater of $5 a share or 30%, so a $6 short needs about 83% maintenance ([FINRA 4210](https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/rulebooks/finra-rules/4210)) [Primary; My calculation]. Rule 201 restricts shorting after a 10% one-day fall, and threshold securities are published when fails persist ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/regsho.htm)) [Primary]. Those lists could supply a dated borrow-stress record where the backtest has only today's file, though I did not check retrieval or history depth [Inference]. Short interest is published on the seventh business day after settlement ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/filing-reporting/regulatory-filing-systems/short-interest/faq)) [Primary], so the days-to-cover test's +0.05 has look-ahead if it used settlement dates, which was not checked.

**One defect is verified.** Alpaca warns that a timed-out submission may have reached the market and should not be resent or treated as cancelled until confirmed, and notes client IDs help tracking, though it does not promise idempotency ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/working-with-orders)) [Primary]. `_send` in `services/agents/auto_trader.py` passes no `client_order_id` and logs any exception, including the 10-second timeout, as `rejected` [Sentinel measured]. The broker method already accepts the argument. Later position reconciliation may correct the position, which I did not trace, but the retirement rules depend on measured fills, so the record should be right before it starts. Paper also randomly partially fills eligible orders 10% of the time and ignores NBBO size ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/paper-trading)) [Primary], so partial fills in the ledger are partly simulator noise. Flips may also meet wash-trade 403s, since `_await_fill` returns when its wait expires ([Alpaca](https://docs.alpaca.markets/docs/user-protection)) [Primary; caller not traced].

## Named algorithms mostly solve problems Sentinel has already tried or lacks

No named method has a net-of-cost result on a book like this, and checking the code removes two items the notes proposed. **The notes call a residual training target the top untested idea; it is not untested.** Trial O2 ran a beta-adjusted target at 0.59 and an industry-relative target at 0.99 against 1.01, with the latter a recorded near miss that halved the long leg's shortfall, and both modes are in `training_target` [Sentinel measured, log era, quintile book]. Blitz and coauthors' residual momentum has good gross evidence, Sharpe 0.90 against 0.45 for US 1926-2009 ([summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=23858)) [Paper via secondary], and the follow-up is not fully independent ([Huij and Lansdorp](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/100801)) [Paper]. But `resmom_12_1` was removed in P2 as redundant, correlating 0.91 with momentum, so adding it back has little support [Sentinel measured]. Only a Fama-French-style residual label remains new, and I found no test of such labels in ML models.

Overlap weighting largely falls away too. The monthly `HorizonSpec` is `("monthly", 21, 21, 21, 21, 12)`, which reads as 21-session labels sampled every 21 sessions, with the module describing such sampling as non-overlapping (`shared/utils/factor_model.py`; field names not read) [Sentinel measured]. The notes' grep did not rule this route out. Uniqueness weights and sequential bootstrap thus have little to do; their support is mechanical anyway, and the vendor's own check concerns the sampler, not returns ([Hudson & Thames](https://hudsonthames.org/?p=3574)) [Vendor]. The same reading satisfies Numerai's advice to treat each era as one observation and purge overlaps ([Numerai](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/data)) [Primary], which the bots notes wanted audited.

**A sharper question comes from the fixed scoring.** Scores moved to simple returns, but training targets and IC stayed on log (`MODEL_TRIALS.md`). A log label ranks volatile names lower by about half their variance, then the book earns the arithmetic return. The model may therefore be drawn to volatile shorts partly by label construction [Inference, untested, and neither source proposes it]. Quintile grades may blunt this for the ranker, but a one-trial test on a simple-return label is cheap and directly addresses the volatile-short concentration.

Daniel and Moskowitz's panic-state conditioning remains the most relevant outside idea. It finds momentum crashes partly forecastable after market declines and high volatility, and dynamic scaling roughly doubling alpha and Sharpe ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)) [Paper]. The notes' claim that damage concentrates on shorts is inferred from the abstract. It is also not entirely unexplored: B5 stress scaling reached 0.14 against B3's -0.01, with a shallower worst month, on a different base and in the log era [Sentinel measured]. That is more encouraging than the earlier report's plain "keep no stress scaling" implied, but about 100 months hold few crash states, and small samples of extreme events invite overfitting ([Queen's paper](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/files/139425062/volatility_scaling_in_momentum_Dec_17.pdf)) [Paper, not read; attribution across two PDFs unconfirmed]. Barroso-Santa-Clara scaling sits close to the failed vol sizing, and managed strategies lowered out-of-sample Sharpe and utility in 72 of 103 cases, though that is time-series scaling rather than name sizing ([JFE](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:138:y:2020:i:1:p:95-117); [counts](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=32455)) [Paper via secondary].

The Qlib zoo tells a US book little. Official tables cover Chinese A-shares, with the maintainers noting limited tuning resources ([Qlib](https://github.com/microsoft/qlib/blob/main/examples/benchmarks/README.md)) [Primary]. DoubleEnsemble's **Sharpe 4.94 claim** is the authors', unreplicated, in A-shares and crypto ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.01265.pdf)) [Author claim, via search summary]; on CSI300 Alpha158 Qlib shows IR 1.34 against LightGBM's 1.02, reversing on Alpha360, and a CSI500 row looks like a table error. These are not like-for-like comparisons. Meta-labeling evidence is proponent-linked, with Joubert tied to the vendor Hudson & Thames per [its repo](https://github.onedrive11.workers.dev/hudson-and-thames/meta-labeling) [Vendor; mirror URL, verify], and the improvements summary is uncited ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta-Labeling)). It is also a learned bet filter, and fixed bet filters just failed, though a learned one is not strictly the same test [Inference]. Garleanu-Pedersen's aim portfolio belongs to the same trading-slowly class as the failed buffer and needs per-signal decay that one blended score lacks ([NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w15205)) [Paper; net Sharpes not retrieved].

## Competitions and live platforms teach validation, not returns

Numerai's docs offer the most transferable recipe: 156-era walk-forward chunks, purges of 8 or 16 eras, per-era neutralisation with a tunable proportion, and blends of target-specific LightGBM models ([Numerai](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/models)) [Primary]. Its pay is not transferable, since 15 x MMC60 rewards uniqueness against a meta-model a solo book does not have ([Numerai](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/staking)) [Primary; Inference]. **Vendor flag:** Numerai One's 25.45% at Sharpe 2.75 for 2024 and JPMorgan's $500M capacity come from the fund's own blog, for a model investor with retrospectively applied fees, with no audit mentioned ([Numerai](https://blog.numer.ai/jpmorgan-secures-500m-capacity/)) [Self-reported]. Press snippets report -17% in 2023 with unconfirmed year attribution ([Benzinga](https://fr.benzinga.com/quote/cryptocurrency/25/08/47341702/numerai-secures-up-to-500m-commitment-from-jpmorgan-asset-management)) [Secondary]. Neutralisation is a tool, not an evidenced gain, and FNC is informational rather than paid ([Numerai](https://docs.numer.ai/numerai-tournament/scoring)) [Primary].

Contest evidence is narrow, and **no Kaggle page was read directly, so I have no measured shake-up statistics**. G-Research's organiser says the top three crypto entries used LightGBM, credits feature work, and reports large rank moves from noise across three months of frozen, live-scored models ([G-Research](https://www.gresearch.com/news/wrapping-up-the-g-research-crypto-forecasting-competition/)) [Primary]. A secondary source describes Jane Street's top solution as XGBoost plus a supervised-autoencoder MLP, with purged CV ([BulletTech](https://bullettech.github.io/BulletTech/Main_Course/Machine_Learning/2022-01-30-Kaggle-exp-s2)) [Secondary]. A leak claim on JPX is from one unopened anecdote ([CSDN](https://blog.youkuaiyun.com/xiuxiuxiu666/article/details/125877329)) [Anecdote]. None of this gives net-of-cost performance, and I found no winning model later traded profitably. It weakly supports keeping trees and no deep stack.

Crowd platforms and verification claims deserve scepticism. Quantopian's Point72 deal of up to $250M was contingent on benchmarks ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2016/07/27/steven-cohen-250-million-quantopian)) [Secondary], and its platform closed in November 2020 after the company said it was not making money ([eFinancialCareers](https://www.efinancialcareers.com/news/2020/11/quantopian-shutdown)) [Secondary]. QuantConnect halted Alpha Streams v1.0 for a rebuild, cause unestablished ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/13441/alpha-streams-refactoring-2-0/)) [Primary]. Collective2 labels its results hypothetical ([support](https://support.collective2.eu/portal/en/kb/articles/why-hypothetical-results-and-performance)) [Self-reported], and NinjaTrader's partner site says it does not audit leaders ([NinjaTrader](https://follow.ninjatrader.com/details/154653200)) [Primary]. An old snapshot of 193 Collective2 stock systems had a median age of 6 months and suggests survivorship, without a mortality rate ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=4128)) [Secondary]. The strongest case is F-Squared: a $35M settlement after pre-inception hypothetical results were presented as actual ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2014-289)) [Primary], with an error inflating 38% to 135% ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2014/12/22/sec-f-squared-fraud)) [Secondary]. Sentinel's record sits around "tracked but hypothetical" on my evidence ladder, so forward returns should never be spliced onto a backtest, including on the dashboard [Inference]. I found no audited postmortem of a small builder, so builder failure stories remain anecdote.

## Conclusion

The literature keeps pointing at a book whose edge is largest exactly where measurement is weakest. Volatile shorts generate nearly all the short return, the log metric overstated them, and borrow may remove what remains. No manual or named algorithm can settle that, because the deciding variable is a historical cost series Sentinel does not have and paper cannot generate. The realistic outcome is that B1 stays a process test for years, and real capital may depend more on a borrow answer than on any model improvement.

The novel implication is about order of work, not about method. The most valuable next steps are re-examining decisions already made, not trying new ideas: selection on log scores, a log training label, and fill records that can misreport a timeout. With about 54 trials against a roughly 0.5 Sharpe, every extra variant raises the deflation bar, so fewer, registered tests are worth more than a longer list.
