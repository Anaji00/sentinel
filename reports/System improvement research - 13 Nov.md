## Addendum 3: is the flat top of the monthly calibration real? (13 Nov, read-only)

Data read: the lab's stored `alpha_calibration` (Redis `sentinel:portfolio_lab`, as_of 2026-11-06, generated 7 Nov 05:51), `MODEL_TRIALS.md`, and `recommendation_log`. I did not rerun the lab (it needs the 6 GB panel; not allowed in this pass), so the sign test below is specified, not run.

**What the lab shows (monthly, 97,544 pairs).** Decile means (raw alpha bps to realised log excess bps): -122.9 to -330; -31.5 to -98; -16.1 to -97; -7.7 to -101; -2.0 to -96; +2.0 to -70; +7.5 to -73; +15.3 to -55; +27.7 to -58; **+72.1 to -11**. So deciles are monotone (rising 320 bps from bottom to top, about +85 bps from the middle deciles to the top), and the flat top lives only in the 1% knots: +55.3 to -8.8 (n 7,804), +104.6 to +3.2 (n 975), **+173.6 to -43.6 (n 976)**. The identical +32.5 bps forecast is a design property: pool-adjacent-violators merges non-monotone tail knots and the map is held flat past the last knot (`expected_returns.py` docstring), so every name above about +55 raw gets one number.

**Artefact or property? Mostly artefact of effective sample size, with one confound.**
1. n by knot is pairs, not independent observations. 97,544 pairs over about 2,800 names per date is about **35 monthly dates**, so a 976-pair knot is about 28 names a date for 35 dates. Names on one date share the market and sector shocks. With a monthly sd of about 15% for the high-volatility names that populate the top (my assumption; the lab does not store it), the standard error of a top-knot mean is about 15% / sqrt(35) = 250 bps. The three top knots span 47 bps (-43.6 to +3.2). They are indistinguishable from each other and from the decile mean, so "flat to negative above +55" is not established.
2. Confound: alpha = IC x sigma x z, so the top alpha bucket mixes high rank with high volatility. High-volatility, lottery-like stocks have tended to lag (the low-volatility anomaly), so some of the flattening can come from sigma, not from the rank. The model's own B1 trial says the short leg earns most of its return in the most volatile names (+1.12% to +0.22% a month without them), which points the same way: volatility is a large part of what the alpha scale picks up.
3. Weekly shows the same shape (top knots -17.5, -26.4, -33.6 against a -15 mean) but the weekly standard error is about 8% / sqrt(152 dates) = 65 bps, so a 16 bps dip is 0.25 standard errors.
4. Overlap: the monthly calibration windows are non-overlapping (35 dates over three years), so overlap is not what inflates it; the cross-sectional clustering within a date is.

**Pre-registered sign test (run it in its own capped container after 06:00 UTC).** For each of the 35 monthly dates compute the mean realised excess of (a) the top decile by raw alpha, (b) the middle deciles 4 to 7, (c) the top decile by rank z alone. Statistic: the date-level difference (a minus b), a one-sided t-test over 35 dates with a block bootstrap of dates. Bar: top beats middle at p under 0.05 and the top-1% knot is not below the 9th-decile mean by more than one standard error. Second test: (c) minus (a); if rank-only beats alpha-ranked at p under 0.10, the sigma multiplier is hurting the top end and the map should be fitted on rank z. Report both whatever the result.
